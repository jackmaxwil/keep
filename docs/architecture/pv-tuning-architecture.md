# Applying PV-Tuning to Recover GLM-5.2's 1-Bit E8-Lattice Routed Experts: An Architecture Decision Document

## TL;DR
- **Build it, but fix the vocabulary first and gate hard on Air.** PV-Tuning (Malinovskii, Mazur, Ilin, Kuznedelev, Burlachenko, Yi, Alistarh & Richtárik, "PV-Tuning: Beyond Straight-Through Estimation for Extreme LLM Compression," arXiv:2405.14852, NeurIPS 2024) is the correct recovery thesis because it perturbs only the values of existing tensors (codes + scales) and is explicitly representation-agnostic, so the E8 safetensors contract and the three MLX Metal kernels survive untouched. Critically, the task brief inverts the paper's naming: in the paper the **P-step optimizes the continuous parameters (scales) by backprop while the V-step updates the discrete codes via nearest-codeword search** — this inversion must be corrected before any implementation.
- **The single largest unproven risk is the fixed 1-bit E8 residual codebook.** Every published PV-Tuning result is on dense Llama/Mistral with *learned* AQLM codebooks or QuIP# E8P at ~2 bits; there is no published PV-Tuning result at 1.0 bpw on a fixed lattice, and none at all on a sparse MoE. With a frozen codebook the P-step has almost nothing continuous left to train except per-group scales, which changes the convergence story and must be settled empirically on GLM-4.5-Air before any GLM-5.2 GPU spend.
- **The ~$1,000 AWS budget is enough for full Air validation plus a first GLM-5.2 expert-subset pass, not a full GLM-5.2 recovery.** Air PV-Tuning fits comfortably; GLM-5.2 must be done by expert-subsetting (most-routed/most-sensitive experts first) with teacher logits supplied off-box from a Q8 checkpoint. A full 256-expert × 75-layer pass is out of budget and out of scope for the first milestone.

## Key Findings

**1. The method maps cleanly onto the E8 representation, but the brief's P/V semantics are reversed.** PV-Tuning formalizes quantized fine-tuning as alternating coordinate descent over a partition P (which group maps to which codeword) and the set of values V (the continuous numbers the codes decode to). Per the paper's Algorithm 3, the **P-step** ("update codebooks by backprop") optimizes continuous parameters with Adam, and the **V-step** ("choose a subspace s and update codes … model.codes[s] = find_nearest(update[s])") performs a discrete nearest-codeword reassignment over a small top-τ subspace of weights selected by largest Adam-update magnitude. The naming is deliberately "inverted": the *P-step fixes the partition P* and therefore optimizes continuous values, while the *V-step fixes the values V* and therefore optimizes the discrete assignments. The brief states the opposite (P updates assignments, V updates continuous); this is a naming inversion that would corrupt any implementation if propagated. The convergence guarantee (Theorem 3.1) only guarantees monotone non-increasing loss, not global optimality.

**2. A fixed, data-free codebook strips the P-step of its most powerful lever.** PV-Tuning's headline gains on AQLM come from backprop into *learned* codebooks. With QuIP#'s E8/E8P the codebook is fixed by construction — it is a structured lattice intersected with a ball, never learned (Tseng, Chee, Sun, Kuleshov & De Sa, arXiv:2402.04396; the Cornell-RelaxML quip-sharp repo confirms `finetune_e2e_llama.py` "tunes the sign vectors (SU/SV), layernorms, and language model head," never the lattice itself). The paper's Appendix C generalization does not name E8P; it covers learned VQ, additive/residual VQ, outliers, and low-rank adapters. So in our setting the P-step's continuous degrees of freedom collapse to **per-group fp16 scales plus any non-expert parameters we elect to touch** — and the recovery must come disproportionately from the V-step's discrete reassignment. This is the central thing Air must prove.

**3. VQ-1.0 (1-bit E8 residual) is outside the paper's demonstrated envelope.** The paper's lowest-bit headline (Table 2 / Appendix L Table 9) is LLaMA-3 8B at 1.01 bpw reaching WikiText-2 ≈ 11.1 versus ≈ 28.8 for the one-shot BiLLM binary baseline — confirming that at ~1 bit the fine-tuning algorithm dominates the representation. But that result is on a *learned* VQ backbone, dense, and the absolute perplexity is still far above FP16's 5.54. There is no published 1.0-bpw PV-Tuning result on a fixed E8 residual lattice. We should expect the V-step (code reassignment) to do most of the work and should not assume the Llama-class magnitudes transfer. (The abstract's own headline claim is narrower: PV-Tuning achieves "the first Pareto-optimal quantization for Llama 2 family models at 2 bits per parameter" — 2-bit, not 1-bit, is where the method is firmly established.)

**4. The MoE coverage problem is real and must be handled as data discipline, not a second method.** Under top-8 routing each of 128 (Air) / 256 (GLM-5.2) experts sees only a sliver of tokens, so under-routed experts receive negligible gradient signal — the inter-expert imbalance documented by MoEQuant (Hu et al., arXiv:2505.03804), EAQuant (arXiv:2506.13329), and EAC-MoE (arXiv:2508.01625). The cheapest faithful mitigation is expert-balanced sampling: bias the calibration stream toward tokens that activate cold experts, with a per-expert minimum token guarantee (MoEQuant's Expert-Balanced Self-Sampling, EBSS). EAC-MoE additionally shows that low-bit quantization induces *expert-shift* (the router picks different experts), which is why the router stays high-precision in this design and why expert-selection change rate is a first-class eval metric.

**5. The format-preservation constraint is satisfiable and is the reason PV-Tuning beats the alternatives here.** PV-Tuning is explicitly compatible with existing inference kernels because it does not change the representation — only the values of the codes and scales. EoRA/MILO low-rank compensators and AlphaQ variable-bit allocation would both alter the on-disk tensor set and force kernel changes, violating constraint 3. This is the decisive architectural reason to choose PV-Tuning.

## Details

### Research Question 1 — P-step over a fixed E8 lattice
With the codebook frozen, formulate the P-step as gradient descent (Adam) on the per-group fp16 scales only, computed by backprop through the dequantization path (decode E8 codes → multiply by scale → undo RHT → matmul). The V-step is the discrete reassignment: for the top-τ groups selected by Adam-update magnitude, compute the target vector (current decoded weight minus the Adam step), then `find_nearest` against the 256-entry E8 codebook *in the RHT-transformed, scale-normalized space* — i.e., the nearest-codeword search must be performed in exactly the space the kernel decodes in, so that the discrete choice the trainer makes is the discrete choice the kernel will reproduce. (Appendix C confirms the V-step "can be done by trying all values in V(x) and selecting the one with the lowest ϕ̂(·)," with the efficient minimum-L2 form in Appendix D.) Because the codebook is fixed, there is no codebook-learning contribution to convergence; the algorithm reduces to "scale tuning + code reassignment," which is weaker than the full AQLM-style PV-Tuning the paper validated. Applicability to the 1-bit E8 residual codebook (VQ-1.0) is **unproven in the literature** — the paper's VQ results and QuIP#'s E8P are both effectively 2-bit. This is the first Air experiment.

### Research Question 2 — V-step parameterization
Trainable continuous parameters, in priority order: (a) **per-group fp16 scales** (always, this is the core P-step lever under a fixed codebook); (b) **a per-expert or global scalar codebook-scale multiplier** — a single extra fp16 number per expert that the kernel already effectively applies via scales, so it does not break the 1 KiB cache-resident codebook property and does not add a new tensor type if folded into the existing scales at export. The RHT sign seeds should **stay frozen**: QuIP# tunes S_U/S_V, but those are stored as seeds in our artifact and making them trainable would require materializing and re-exporting sign vectors, risking the format contract; freezing them is the conservative default and an Air ablation can test whether unfreezing buys anything. The shared global codebook must **never** become trainable — that is the one object whose immutability guarantees kernel and cache invariance. Non-expert affine tensors (norms, router, shared expert) stay high-precision and are *not* PV-targets; they may participate in the forward pass but are frozen. (The paper notes it also reuses gradients to update non-quantized parameters — embeddings, normalization, biases — but in our single-thesis design those stay frozen to preserve the high-precision guarantee on everything outside the routed experts.)

### Research Question 3 — Train/inference stack seam
Training happens in PyTorch off-box; serving is MLX/Metal on the Mac. The seam is the safetensors contract: `*.codes` (uint8/uint16), `*.scales` (fp16), one shared `e8_codebook` (uint32[256]), RHT sign seeds, `quantization_config.json`. The numerical-parity gate: after export, load the PyTorch-trained checkpoint in MLX and run a fixed prompt batch through both stacks, requiring **logit cosine ≥ 0.99999 and hidden-state cosine ≥ 0.9999** against the PyTorch dequant reference — the same bar the existing kernels already pass. Pitfalls: (a) **endianness/packing** — the uint8 code packing `[out, in/8]` must be byte-identical; verify the PyTorch trainer writes codes in the exact bit order the Metal `gather_vqmm` kernel reads; (b) **dtype** — scales must round-trip as fp16, not bf16, or the decode drifts; (c) **E8 sign/shift decode** — E8P uses 8 table bits + 7 sign-flip bits + 1 parity-shift bit (per the QuIP# decode example: "the first 8 bits … indicate … the entry in S … the next 7 bits … negate … since we need an odd number of sign flips, the 8th from right bit is also a sign flip"), so any `find_nearest` in PyTorch must encode to the identical sign/parity convention the kernel decodes. This is the most likely silent-corruption source and must be unit-tested against the kernel's decode on random codes.

### Research Question 4 — Expert coverage / balanced sampling
Treat as a training-data discipline inside PV-Tuning. Regimen: (a) profile expert activation frequency on a natural calibration stream (RedPajama-like + a coding/agentic slice matching GLM-5.2's domain); (b) for cold experts below a per-expert token floor, oversample tokens that route to them (MoEQuant-style Expert-Balanced Self-Sampling) or, as a fallback, use forced/over-routing (raise effective top-k for calibration only, as GPTQModel's `ExpertsRoutingOverride`/`ExpertsRoutingBypass` controls do) to guarantee every expert sees a minimum gradient signal. **Default token budget:** size the calibration set so each expert sees a floor of activated tokens; with 128 (Air) experts and top-8 routing a 1M-token batch (the paper's batch size of 2²⁰ tokens) gives ~62k expert-activations per expert on average but a long tail of cold experts — the floor guarantee is what matters, not the average. Keep natural routing as the primary signal and only force-route for the cold tail, because forced routing changes the activation distribution the expert actually sees in deployment.

### Research Question 5 — Teacher and loss objective
**Default: KL-distillation from the Q8 teacher (quantization-aware distillation, QAD).** This is both what PV-Tuning actually used at scale (Appendix K: "We minimize Kullback–Leibler divergence … to approximate the predictions (logits) of a 'teacher' — the same model prior to quantization," on the official 1B-token RedPajama sample, batch size 2²⁰ ≈ 1M tokens, sequence length 4096 for Llama-2 / 8192 for Llama-3) and what the NVIDIA Nemotron QAD report independently found best: "Table 8 shows that KL divergence consistently outperforms MSE across benchmarks … better suited to measure distributional differences and provides better gradients for probability matching" (arXiv:2601.20088, *Quantization-Aware Distillation for NVFP4 Inference Accuracy Recovery*). That report also found QAD "robust to incomplete data coverage" — "QAD trained with math-only or coding-only data achieves near-full data performance, demonstrating cross-domain knowledge transfer" — and that "using the original BF16 model as teacher is more effective than a larger teacher," which supports using the same-family Q8 GLM-5.2 rather than any larger surrogate. The brief's premise that PV-Tuning used "cross-entropy on calibration text" is only half right — §3 motivates with CE, but the large-scale runs use KL to teacher logits. **Escalation:** if logit-only KL underperforms on the coding/agentic tail, add a hidden-state (feature) distillation term on the residual stream. **Global vs block-wise:** start block-wise/layer-local (QuIP#-style intra-block reconstruction) for memory and per-expert coverage — block-local lets you fit the gradient on one GPU and concentrate signal on the experts in that block — then escalate to global end-to-end KL for final polish if Air shows block-local leaves quality on the table. The DSA indexer, MLA, router, and shared expert all stay high-precision and participate in the teacher forward pass unchanged; only the routed-expert FFN codes/scales are trainable, so teacher and student differ *only* in the routed-expert weights.

### Research Question 6 — Compute architecture on ~$1,000 AWS
Concrete instances (June 2026 pricing, spot strongly preferred). On AWS, after the June 2025 ~44% H100 price cut, `p5.48xlarge` (8×H100 80GB) sits around $33–34/hr on-demand (~$4.1/GPU-hr) in lower-priced regions, with list rates as high as $55.04/hr (~$6.88/GPU) in us-east-1 (per CloudPrice/Vantage and IntuitionLabs' June 2026 comparison). AWS P5 spot saves roughly 50–70% off on-demand (Wring's AWS GPU pricing guide). If AWS spot capacity is contended, neocloud H100 floors are materially cheaper — Thunder Compute $1.38/GPU-hr, Vast.ai $1.87/GPU-hr, RunPod $1.99/GPU-hr (June 2026) — and are spot-friendly with per-second billing; the design should be portable to whichever has capacity. **Air:** Air's routed experts are ~100B params; the PV-Tuning paper trained 70B Llama on a single 8×A100 node, so full Air PV-Tuning fits on one 8×H100/8×A100 node with sharding + gradient checkpointing + optimizer offload. Budget a few hundred GPU-hours: at a ~$1.4–2/GPU-hr spot rate × 8 GPUs × ~24h ≈ $270–380 for a full Air pass with margin. **GLM-5.2:** a full pass is infeasible on $1,000 — 725B of routed experts is ~10× Air. Use **expert-subsetting**: rank experts by routing frequency × quantization sensitivity (KLD contribution), PV-tune the top slice first (the most-routed experts that cover the bulk of coding/agentic traffic), expand as coding evals demand. Per the paper, PV-Tuning costs ~1.5× an STE fine-tune in wall-clock and needs extra memory to hold ∇ϕ. The teacher forward (Q8 GLM-5.2, ~805 GB) must run on a multi-GPU node (8×H200, 1128 GB) off-box; precompute and cache teacher logits/hidden states for the calibration set in one batch job to avoid paying for an idle teacher during student training. **Verdict:** $1,000 plausibly covers full Air validation (~$300) plus a first GLM-5.2 expert-subset pass with cached teacher logits (~$500–700); it does not cover a full GLM-5.2 recovery or repeated global end-to-end passes.

### Research Question 7 — Air → GLM-5.2 scaling plan
**Transfers unchanged:** the P/V step formulation, the fixed-E8 nearest-codeword V-step, the safetensors contract and parity gate, the KL-teacher objective, expert-balanced sampling, block-local→global escalation. **Must change:** 256 vs 128 experts (2× the discrete search surface; on the order of 19,200 routed-expert projections across 75 sparse layers vs Air's ~5,760 across 45); the **DSA indexer (21 full + 57 shared indexer layers) and MLA attention stay high-precision** and are absent from Air's simpler `glm4_moe` attention, so the GLM-5.2 forward must correctly run DSA top-k selection during teacher/student passes — a code path Air does not exercise; the **MTP layer** (present in both, must be excluded from quantization); larger hidden (6144 vs 4096) and moe_intermediate (2048 vs 1408); and the **1M-context KV reality** — calibration sequences need not be 1M tokens (the paper used 4–8k), but the DSA indexer must be exercised on long-enough sequences that its top-k behavior is representative. **Go/no-go gate authorizing GLM-5.2 GPU-days:** Air must hit its quality thresholds (below) *and* demonstrate stable PV-Tuning convergence on the fixed 1-bit E8 codebook with the parity gate passing end-to-end.

### Research Question 8 — Validation protocol and metrics
Eval battery against the Q8 baseline (not FP16, since Q8 is the near-lossless teacher), Air first then GLM-5.2, in gating order: (1) **mean KLD** vs Q8 teacher on held-out calibration text — primary convergence signal; (2) **99.9th-percentile KLD** — weight the tail heavily because GLM-5.2 is coding/agentic-first and sub-2-bit damage concentrates in the tail; (3) **top-1 token agreement** with the unquantized router; (4) **expert-selection change rate** vs the unquantized router (the EAC-MoE expert-shift metric) — must stay low or routing degrades regardless of per-expert reconstruction quality; (5) **coding/agentic smoke evals** — Terminal-Bench-style, SWE-style patch generation, long-context tool prompts. Concrete go/no-go thresholds (calibrated on the first Air RTN baseline, stated as targets): PV-tuning must reduce mean KLD vs the RTN-only baseline by a decisive margin and bring 99.9th-pct KLD down materially; expert-selection change rate should improve or hold versus RTN; coding smoke evals must not regress versus RTN and should recover a meaningful fraction of the Q8→RTN gap. Air gates GLM-5.2: no GLM-5.2 GPU spend until Air clears all five.

### Research Question 9 — Risk register
- **Fixed-codebook P-step on a 1-bit residual lattice underdelivers.** *Cheapest Air kill experiment:* on a single Air MoE block, run PV-Tuning with only scales trainable + V-step reassignment at VQ-1.0; if block-local KLD barely improves over RTN, the thesis is dead at 1-bit and you fall back to VQ-2.0 (E8P) where the literature has support.
- **RHT interaction with the discrete update.** The `find_nearest` must operate in the RHT-rotated, scaled space or the trainer and kernel disagree. *Air experiment:* unit-test `find_nearest` encode against the Metal decode on random codes; require bit-identical round-trip before any training.
- **P-step cost/stability across GLM-5.2's ~19,200 expert projections.** *Air proxy:* measure wall-clock and memory per PV iteration on Air's ~5,760 projections and extrapolate; if the V-step's top-τ search dominates, cap τ per matrix (the paper sets τ by the 0.01 trust-ratio rule, with LR 3e-4 for the P-step and 3e-3 for the codes).
- **DSA-MoE forward differs from dense-Llama validation setting.** *Air limitation:* Air has no DSA, so DSA correctness in the teacher/student forward can only be validated on GLM-5.2 itself — flag this as a residual GLM-5.2-only risk and budget a small DSA-forward parity check before the expert-subset pass.
- **PyTorch↔MLX parity drift.** *Air experiment:* the ≥0.99999 logit-cosine gate on a fixed prompt batch, run after every export.
- **Calibration/coverage failure (cold experts).** *Air experiment:* measure per-expert token counts under natural routing; if the cold tail is large, validate that expert-balanced oversampling closes the per-expert KLD gap without distorting routing.

### Research Question 10 — Recommended architecture + build sequence
**Recommended design:** PV-Tuning with a frozen E8 codebook, training only per-group fp16 scales (P-step) + discrete code reassignment in the RHT-scaled space (V-step), KL-distillation from a Q8 teacher run off-box, block-local reconstruction escalating to global KL, expert-balanced calibration sampling with a per-expert token floor, RHT seeds and the shared codebook frozen, all non-expert tensors high-precision and frozen. Default operating point VQ-1.0, with VQ-2.0 (E8P) as the documented fallback if 1-bit recovery fails on Air. Optionally combine the V-step with STE (the paper found PV+STE "slightly better … in most setups").

**Dependency-ordered milestones (Air end-to-end before any GLM-5.2 work):**
1. Correct the P/V naming inversion in the design; finish the streaming RTN converter to produce the `glm-4.5-air-vq` artifact.
2. Unit-test `find_nearest`↔Metal-decode bit-parity on random E8 codes.
3. Single-block Air PV-Tuning at VQ-1.0; measure block-local KLD vs RTN. **Kill gate.**
4. Full Air PV-Tuning; run the five-metric eval battery vs Q8. **Go/no-go gate.**
5. Build the off-box Q8 GLM-5.2 teacher-logit cache for the calibration set (one batch job).
6. GLM-5.2 expert-subset PV-Tuning on the most-routed/most-sensitive experts; DSA-forward parity check; partial eval battery.

**Open questions only Air can resolve:** Does a frozen 1-bit E8 codebook leave enough continuous signal for the P-step, or must we fall back to VQ-2.0? Does the V-step's discrete reassignment converge stably at 1-bit without STE assistance (and does the PV+STE advantage hold on a fixed lattice)? How large is Air's cold-expert tail and how much oversampling closes it? Does block-local reconstruction suffice or is global KL required?

## Recommendations
1. **Immediately correct the P/V semantics** in all design docs (P = continuous/scales by backprop; V = discrete codes by nearest-codeword search) — this is a one-line fix that prevents a class of implementation bugs.
2. **Run the single-block Air kill experiment (milestone 3) before anything else funded.** It costs a few GPU-hours and definitively answers whether 1-bit fixed-E8 recovery is viable. If it fails, switch the default to VQ-2.0 and re-baseline — do not spend on GLM-5.2.
3. **Default to KL-from-Q8 distillation, not cross-entropy**, matching both PV-Tuning's actual large-scale recipe and the NVFP4 QAD finding that KL beats MSE and tolerates partial-domain calibration; hold hidden-state distillation in reserve for the coding tail.
4. **Pre-cache teacher logits/hidden states off-box in one batch job** to keep the expensive Q8 GLM-5.2 teacher from idling against the $1,000 budget — idle instance time is the budget killer.
5. **Gate GLM-5.2 spend strictly on the five Air metrics**, weighting 99.9th-percentile KLD and expert-selection change rate because the model is coding/agentic-first.

**Thresholds that change the plan:** if the single-block Air experiment shows no decisive KLD improvement over RTN at 1-bit → abandon VQ-1.0, move to VQ-2.0. If expert-selection change rate stays high after PV-tuning → the problem is routing/expert-shift, not reconstruction, and the fix is calibration coverage, not more training. If block-local KLD plateaus far above the Q8 teacher → escalate to global end-to-end KL before scaling to GLM-5.2.

## Caveats
- **No published PV-Tuning result exists for a fixed E8 codebook at 1.0 bpw, nor for any sparse MoE.** Every quantitative PV-Tuning number cited here is from dense Llama/Mistral with learned AQLM codebooks or 2-bit QuIP# E8P. The transfer to a fixed 1-bit lattice on a DSA-MoE is genuinely unproven; the Air fixture is the only way to settle it, and the brief's own framing should be treated as a hypothesis, not a result.
- **The AQLM/PV-Tuning public codebase officially supports only LLaMA, Mistral, and Mixtral families** (per the repo README: "This repository is currently designed to work with models of LLaMA, Mistral and Mixtral families"). Mixtral support proves the code can handle MoE routing, but GLM's `glm_moe_dsa` / `glm4_moe` architectures, MLA, and DSA are not supported out of the box — a non-trivial porting effort is implied and is not de-risked by the Mixtral precedent.
- **The PV-Tuning paper contains internal numeric inconsistencies** (e.g., the LLaMA-3 8B 1.01-bit WikiText-2 figure appears as both 11.13 and 11.17, and a BiLLM C4 figure as both 65.0 and 257 across Table 2 and Table 9), so treat its absolute numbers as approximate.
- **AWS pricing is volatile and spot capacity for H100/H200 is contended during business hours**; the cost estimates assume spot availability and aggressive checkpointing, and a sustained on-demand fallback (~$33–55/hr for 8×H100) would blow the $1,000 budget. Keep the job portable to neocloud spot (Thunder/Vast/RunPod ~$1.4–2/GPU-hr) as a hedge.
- **DSA correctness in the training forward pass cannot be validated on Air** (Air has no DSA indexer), leaving a residual GLM-5.2-only integration risk that must be checked directly on GLM-5.2 before the expert-subset pass.
- Some architecture facts (GLM-5.2 layer/expert counts, DSA indexer split, 1M-context/IndexShare) are taken as given from the brief and corroborated against Z.ai/HuggingFace `glm_moe_dsa` config documentation; the 1M-context and IndexShare details are secondary-source and should be confirmed against the shipping `config.json`.
