# Calibration and held-out data for REAP pruning of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017

Date: 2026-11-05. Scope: web research only. Every Hugging Face dataset id below
was checked against `https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/<id>` on 2026-11-05/06
unless it is marked **UNVERIFIED** or is hosted elsewhere.

## 0. Target model facts (from the HF config, verified)

`deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017` (sha `1bae0e10...`, MIT, modified
2026-10-17): 40 layers, `hidden_size` 5120, `moe_intermediate_size` 2304, 384
routed experts plus 1 shared expert, top-6, `sqrtsoftplus` scoring with
`noaux_tc`, FP8 dense weights with FP4 experts (block 32x32, ue8m0 scales), and
a 64K native context YaRN-scaled to 1M. The config also carries 3 MTP/"dspark"
layers that have their own 128-expert MoE.

Implications for calibration:

- An expert gets about 6/384 = 1.56% of tokens on average, so 3M calibration
  tokens give about 47K routed tokens per expert per layer. Rare experts can get
  less than 10% of that.
- The second moment of an expert's gate/up input is a 5120x5120 matrix, and
  down_proj's input is 2304-dim. A well-conditioned estimate needs many more
  tokens than d_in for each expert. Dense 2-bit methods use 25-35M tokens per
  model (QuIP#: 6144 x 4096; QTIP Llama-3: 4096 x 8192) [Q1, Q2].

## 1. State of the art: calibration data for MoE expert pruning and PTQ

### 1.1 What REAP itself did (arXiv 2510.13999, ICLR 2026) [R1, R2]

- **Models up to about 110B:** 1,024 samples packed to 2,048 tokens.
- **Models of about 110B and up** (Qwen3-Coder-480B, Kimi-K2): 12,228 samples
  with max length 16,384, no truncation and no packing. The mix includes
  evol-codealpaca, `Salesforce/xlam-function-calling-60k`, and
  `SWE-bench/SWE-smith-trajectories`.
- **Reasoning models** add 12,288 samples from `open-r1/Mixture-of-Thoughts`
  (4,096 each from code, math and science).
- **Repo composite recipe** (24,576 samples, max 16K): 4,096 evol-codealpaca,
  12,288 Mixture-of-Thoughts, 4,096 xLAM, and 4,096 from the SWE-smith `tool`
  split. It uses `dataset:samples` syntax.
- **Chat formatting:** the default chat template is applied, and `</think>` is
  closed for hybrid models.
- **Domain matters a lot for generative tasks.** Calibrating on C4 made code
  generation "collapse": many pruned models emitted incoherent text and scored
  0%. On multiple-choice tasks C4 was equal or slightly better, which is why MC
  benchmarks hide the damage. Domain-matched calibration helps most on
  fine-grained MoEs (Qwen3-30B, ERNIE). A combined code + writing + math set gave
  the best all-round retention for REAP.
- Cerebras GLM-4.6 REAP cards list the same three sources (evol-codealpaca,
  xLAM, SWE-smith-trajectories) [R3].

### 1.2 Later work (2025-2026)

| Paper | Calibration finding relevant to us |
|---|---|
| **EAN / MC-Suite**, Jaiswal et al., arXiv 2504.05586 [P1] | Among 16 criteria, the expert activation norm (EAN) was strongest. Expert dropping mainly hurts instruction following, and k-shot examples or SFT recover it. |
| **EASY-EP**, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2504.06792 [P2] | On DeepSeek-R1 and V3: 25 in-domain demonstrations recover about 99% of critical experts, and 5 recover over 90%. Input plus the model's **own generated output** beat inputs only, outputs only, inputs plus gold answer, and pretraining/CommonCrawl text (CommonCrawl scored about 0 on AIME). Mixed-domain pruning averages normalized per-domain scores. |
| **MoEQuant**, ICML 2025, arXiv 2505.03804 [P3] | Standard calibration sets give long-tailed expert coverage, so under-routed experts get too few calibration tokens and large quantization error. Expert-balanced self-sampling, where the model generates its own data, gave more than 10 points on HumanEval for DeepSeekMoE-16B at 4-bit. |
| **AIMER**, arXiv 2603.18492 (Apr 2026) [P4] | For REAP on Qwen3-30B at 50%, results were "highly sensitive to calibration set size even with the same corpus" across 0.5M to 2.1M C4 tokens. Baselines used 4.2M C4 tokens. REAP calibration took 0.75 to 3 hours on 7-30B models. |
| **HOPE**, Tseng et al. (AWS), arXiv 2609.18916 (Sep 2026) [P5] | Second-order expert pruning (REAP is its first-order special case). Calibrated on all 111K Evol-CodeAlpaca prompts, or on full **SWE-bench Verified agent trajectories** (tool calls plus observations, truncated at 100K tokens) generated by the unpruned model. REAP needed about 8K prompts to reach 99% prune-set stability. Calibration-set Jaccard between the two sets was about 0.6. On SWE-Bench Pro, the agentic calibration scored higher for REAP **at every pruning rate**, and for HOPE at 50%. |
| **Robust calibration for domain-general pruning**, arXiv 2610.15578 (Oct 2026) [P6] | On gpt-oss-20b, a default of 64 sequences was enough, and scaling beyond small sizes gave little gain. English-only calibration transferred across 7 languages. Diverse pretraining-like data matched test-distribution data. Caveat: code was **not** evaluated and results are preliminary. |
| **Generic TB-Coverage**, arXiv 2607.01710 [P7] | Profiles experts per corpus and keeps a coverage budget per corpus before the global mask. Generic corpora only; it beat REAP on zero-shot MC. |
| **OptEP**, arXiv 2610.27123 [P8] | Reconstruction-optimal subset selection with task-domain calibration. Its gains are largest under aggressive pruning. |
| **Post-compression adjustment**, arXiv 2609.06076 [P9] | 3,000 C4 examples and 1 epoch of fine-tuning recover 37% of the pruning gap. This is relevant if we fine-tune after REAP. |
| **Self-calibration**, Williams et al., NAACL 2025, arXiv 2410.17170 [P10] | Model-generated calibration (BOS-conditioned, temperature 1.0) is competitive with real data and often better for pruning. Quantization was less sensitive to calibration source. Self-generated text is less diverse. |
| **Beware of Calibration Data**, Ji et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.17711 [P11] | At high sparsity, calibration data can matter more than the pruning method. Self-generated data with a perplexity filter helps. |
| **On the Impact of Calibration Data in PTQ and Pruning**, Williams & Aletras, ACL 2024, arXiv 2311.09755 [P12] | Calibration source shifts downstream results substantially, more so for pruning than for quantization. |

### 1.3 Synthesis

1. **Domain-specific calibration is mandatory for code and agentic use.** REAP
   saw C4 collapse code output, and EASY-EP saw CommonCrawl collapse reasoning.
   MC-only evals hide this, so held-out evals must be generative or logit-based
   on the target domains.
2. **Agentic multi-turn trajectories should be the largest slice.** HOPE is the
   only direct comparison. There, SWE-trajectory calibration beat
   Evol-CodeAlpaca for REAP on SWE-Bench Pro at every rate tested, and the two
   sets choose about 40% different experts. REAP's own large-model and agentic
   checkpoints added SWE-smith tool trajectories. The evidence is suggestive, not
   overwhelming: some lower-rate cells favored plain code for other criteria. So
   keep a plain-code slice too.
3. **Self-generated data beats human data when it is "prompt + the model's own
   response".** EASY-EP (on DeepSeek-V3 and R1, our closest architecture
   family), MoEQuant, Self-calibration and Ji et al. all agree. The reason is
   that the router sees the distribution it will see at inference. HOPE also
   generated its agentic traces with the unpruned model. For agent traces, where
   observations come from an environment, the practical proxy is traces from a
   sibling model. `nvidia/Open-SWE-Traces` v1.1 contains about 50K OpenHands
   trajectories from **DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731**.
4. **Size.** Pruning decisions stabilize quickly (64 sequences in [P6], 25 shots
   per domain in [P2]), but REAP with fine-grained experts shows size
   sensitivity between 0.5M and 2.1M tokens [P4]. Production REAP runs use
   12K-25K samples at 16K max, which is tens of millions of tokens. With 384
   experts, the binding constraint is **per-expert coverage**, both for REAP
   saliency means and especially for the 2-bit second moments. A 2-4M token
   budget is fine for saliency ranking but is at the low end for per-expert
   Hessians.
5. **Sequence length.** REAP uses 16,384 for large models with no packing, and
   HOPE uses up to 100K for agent traces. Agentic routing depends on deep
   context (tool outputs and file contents late in the trajectory), so
   2,048-token packing under-represents it.

## 3. Recommended mix

### 3.1 Principles

- **Format.** Render everything with V4.1-Flash's own chat template and tool-call
  format. Convert Hermes/OpenHands/terminus tool calls into the model's native
  tool schema; otherwise the router sees foreign control tokens. Match the
  thinking mode to deployment. If both modes are used, take about 60% thinking
  and 40% non-thinking.
- **Self-generation.** For prompt-type domains (English, tool use, cyber Q&A,
  networking Q&A, business-doc QA), keep the human or source prompts and
  **regenerate the assistant turns with V4.1-Flash itself**. You already run the
  teacher forward pass, so the cost is generation time only. For agentic traces,
  prefer the DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 trajectories in Open-SWE-Traces. Keep raw
  documents (RFCs, code files, advisories, filings) human-written: in agent use
  they arrive as tool observations the router must handle.
- **Sequence length.** Use **max 16,384** tokens, with no packing across
  conversations, as REAP does for its large models. For agent trajectories
  longer than 16K, take message-aligned windows that keep the system prompt and
  tools header plus a random contiguous span of later turns, not just the first
  16K. Add a **long-context slice of about 10% of tokens at 32K-64K** (the
  model's native 64K pre-YaRN) from Open-SWE-Traces, so that long-horizon routing
  is represented, as HOPE did with traces up to 100K. Pack short documents
  (advisories, Q&A) to 4K-8K with EOS separators.
- **Coverage gate.** After the forward pass, log routed-token counts for each
  (layer, expert). For REAP, require at least 1K routed tokens for every expert
  you might keep. For the 2-bit second moments, require at least about 2 x d_in
  (about 10K tokens for gate/up, about 5K for down) for every **kept** expert.
  If any kept expert falls short, top up with more agentic traces, or with
  MoEQuant-style self-sampling steered toward under-covered experts, before
  computing Hessians.
- **Seeds.** As REAP did, check the stability of the prune set across 2
  calibration seeds or subsamples (Jaccard). HOPE found about 0.6 between
  different domain sets.

### 3.2 Calibration split (~3.2M tokens)

| Domain | Tokens | Sources (in priority order) |
|---|---|---|
| Agentic coding (Python, TS, Rust) | 1,000K | Open-SWE-Traces v1.1 DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 OpenHands: Python 400K / TS 300K / Rust 300K. Fill Rust/TS gaps from v1.0/v1.2 or SWE-smith-trajectories. Include the 32K-64K long windows here. |
| Plain code + real diffs | 400K | SWE-rebench-V2-PRs (issue -> patch) 200K, plus stackv2_edu_filtered `.py/.rs/.ts` files 200K, split evenly across the three languages. |
| Bash / terminal agent | 400K | Nemotron-Terminal-Corpus `skill_based_*` 250K, plus AgentTrove (`nl2bash`/`staqc`, reward 1, non-OpenAI teachers) 150K. |
| General tool use | 450K | Toucan-1.5M `multi-turn` 250K, plus Nemotron-SFT-Agentic-v4 / Nemotron-Agentic-v1 150K, plus xLAM single-turn 50K. |
| Cybersecurity | 300K | Primus-FineWeb 150K, CIRCL vulnerability-scores (train split) 75K, and security.stackexchange 75K (prompts with self-generated answers). |
| Networking | 250K | Recent RFCs 150K, plus networkengineering/serverfault/unix StackExchange 100K. |
| Business documents | 250K | finepdfs eng_Latn business-filtered 150K, SEC 10-K/10-Q sections 2023-2026 60K, Enron 40K. |
| English general | 150K | WildChat English prompts with V4.1-Flash responses 100K, plus finewiki en 50K. |
| **Total** | **~3.2M** | About 60% agentic or tool-loop tokens, which matches the target use. |

Rationale:

- Agentic and tool-loop data gets the largest share because HOPE showed
  agentic calibration helps REAP on SWE-Bench Pro at every rate, and REAP's
  agentic checkpoints use it.
- Every target domain gets at least 150K tokens, because REAP and EASY-EP show
  that a domain absent from calibration loses its experts. EASY-EP's 25-shot
  result suggests 150K tokens is far above the minimum for ranking.
- The total stays within 2-4M because [P6] and [P2] show ranking saturates
  early. AIMER's 0.5M-2.1M sensitivity argues against going below about 2M.
- Before quantization, consider growing the agentic slice to about 8-10M tokens
  for Hessian accuracy alone (QTIP/QuIP# scale). That data need not enter the
  REAP saliency pass.

### 3.3 Held-out eval split (disjoint), for teacher top-k logits

Use about 40-60K tokens per domain (~450K total, 100-300 sequences per domain),
with the same template and lengths. Make each split disjoint as follows:

- **Agentic coding:** disjoint **by repository** from calibration, not just by
  instance. Use Open-SWE-Traces repos held out, plus CoderForge `filtered_reward1`
  Python repos not in calibration. CoderForge's different teacher and harness
  also give a robustness check. Seed Rust/TS agent rollouts from SWE-smith-rs/ts
  tasks generated by the teacher.
- **Bash:** a different `skill` taxonomy branch, or a different AgentTrove
  `original_source`.
- **Tool use:** held-out MCP servers in Toucan (split by `target_tools` server).
- **Cyber:** CIRCL's own `test` split, which is group-split by CVE/alias.
- **Networking:** a held-out set of RFC numbers, plus StackExchange questions
  created after the calibration cutoff date.
- **Business:** held-out URL domains in finepdfs, plus different SEC filers.
- **English:** held-out WildChat conversation ids.

Also report per-domain KL against the teacher's top-k, not only accuracy. The
REAP finding that MC accuracy hides generative collapse applies here.
